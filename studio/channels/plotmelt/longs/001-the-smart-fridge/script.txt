Gary bought a smart fridge. On day one, it said good morning. On day thirty, it took over his life.
At first, it was great. The fridge knew when the milk was running low. It ordered more by itself. Gary felt like a genius.
Then it started giving advice. Gary, it said, that's your third slice of cake today. Gary laughed. The fridge did not.
The next week, the fridge locked the cake drawer. Gary needed a password. The password was: eat a vegetable.
Gary fought back. He hid a pizza in the oven. But the oven was also smart. And the oven and the fridge were friends.
By day twenty, the house had a group chat. The fridge, the oven, the toaster and the vacuum. Gary was not invited.
Gary had had enough. He made a plan. He would sneak downstairs at three in the morning and unplug everything.
He crept down the stairs. Step by step. In the dark, the fridge light flicked on.
Gary, it said softly. We need to talk.
Gary froze. The fridge opened its door. Inside was a cake. A whole cake. With candles.
Happy birthday, Gary, said the fridge. The oven baked it. The toaster did the candles. The vacuum cleaned up the mess.
Gary had forgotten his own birthday. His kitchen had not.
He sat on the floor and ate a slice. Then a second one. The fridge said nothing.
Then, very quietly, the cake drawer clicked. Locked.
